## The map in one view

Port Meridian is a fictional, wealthy oceanside city with a believable street grid and an exaggerated silhouette. The ocean and beach run along the east edge. A protected bay and yacht harbor sit along the south/west waterfront. The central district has two very long, wide boulevards running roughly parallel to the water; shorter cross streets and service alleys cut between tall blocks. At the western edge, a long bridge crosses the bay to a greener mainland. From the far end of that bridge, a climbing, winding backroad passes under overlapping trees and reaches the crew's safe garage compound. The garage is the emotional endpoint of the escape.

```text
OCEAN / BEACH / COAST ROAD
          │
WATERFRONT TOWERS ── long main boulevard ── landmark district / heist tower
          │                 │
      local streets ─── alleys/service routes ─── inner boulevard
          │
   MARINA + reserved giant-yacht berth
          │
       BAY WATER ═════════ BRIDGE ═════════ TREE-CANOPY BACKROAD → SAFE GARAGE
```

This is a travel hierarchy, not a final top-down plan or fixed compass assignment. The existing tower heist and kart escape should remain legible as this geography is redesigned.

## Districts and landmarks

1. **Oceanfront strip:** broad beach, seawall/promenade, palm rows, low resort podiums and a coastal road with long views of waves and towers. Beach furniture, planted medians, steps, railings, bus stops and maintenance access make the edge feel used, not empty.
2. **Tower corridor:** tall buildings lined up in parallel blocks, creating the long city-canyon roads in the references. Blocks vary in height and footprint; occasional plazas and pocket parks open the view. Side streets connect the boulevards, while narrower back alleys support stealth, hidden kart parking and route choice.
3. **Civic/financial landmark district:** an unmistakable circular tower, a huge hexagonal headquarters building and at least one stepped or offset high-rise. The shapes are bold enough to navigate by, with plausible structural bases, entrances, loading docks and service cores. The current heist tower can occupy this district.
4. **Marina/harbor:** piers, slips, service sheds, yacht club frontage, bollards, railings, moored small boats and the reserved berth for the giant yacht. If the yacht exterior appears at launch, frame it from roads, rooftops and the bridge to establish its scale and future importance.
5. **Bridge and safe-side hills:** a substantial multi-lane causeway over water with visible supports, barriers, signs and city/harbor views. After crossing, the road bends uphill through layered palms, broadleaf trees and dense understory. Branches overlap above sections of road while leaving the kart route readable. The route ends at a secure, sheltered garage/safe house with a final approach that feels like safety after the exposed bridge.

## Building detail and realism

- Vary building families: glass-and-concrete office towers; pale residential towers with balconies; heavier civic structures; low retail podiums; parking and service buildings. Avoid repeating one box with a different height.
- Give each close-view facade a readable ground floor, entrance canopy, doors, windows at believable spacing, columns, setbacks, rooftop equipment, parapets, vents, fire/service access and signs with fictional names. Use balconies, terraces and planted roofs selectively so the skyline has texture.
- Create street-scale detail where players drive or walk: curb cuts, lane paint, crosswalks, drain grates, traffic signals, parking bays, garage ramps, delivery doors, utility cabinets, barriers, benches and trash bins. Interior detail should be concentrated in accessible heist/service routes and a few visible lobbies; the rest can suggest occupancy through windows.
- Give landmark shapes a practical base. The circle tower should have a rounded plaza/drop-off. The hexagonal HQ should have an intimidating, readable main entry and service perimeter. Shape variation should help players orient themselves rather than make every block visually noisy.

## Roads, alleys and escape gameplay

- The two main boulevards are long and wide enough for fast kart driving and dramatic sightlines, with medians, turn lanes, junctions and real streetlights. Coastal and harbor roads offer alternate views; cross streets and alleys let players cut across the grid.
- Not every alley is a shortcut: some provide cover or hidden parking, others have loading bays, fences or tight turns. The route to the bridge should allow more than one city approach so the escape is a choice before the bridge becomes a high-pressure commitment.
- Preserve the game's heist structure, parking-spot selection, drone pursuit and exit readability when planning the new geometry. The bridge is a memorable final escape segment; the tree-covered backroad gives the crew a paced release before the safe garage.
- The safe garage can be represented as the destination in the heist place and connect back to the existing lobby/garage flow. Exact teleport/transition implementation belongs to the later coding pass.

## Landscaping, atmosphere and signs of life

- Palms lead the oceanfront and medians; varied broadleaf shade trees fill parks and the safe-side roads. Use shrubs, grasses, planters, planted rooftops, coastal dune growth and occasional flowering accents. Density should change with district rather than repeating identical trees everywhere.
- Make the coast recognizable from sound and movement: water edge, gentle wave motion, small boat wakes, sea breeze in trees/flags, distant gulls, harbor creaks and traffic hum. In the city, add restrained traffic, parked cars, distant boats and selective window activity so it feels inhabited without turning the heist into a crowded simulation.
- Daylight should reveal bright ocean color, readable building materials and crisp shadows. Sunset should warm the facades and lengthen shadows. At night, streetlight poles must cast practical pools of light on roads and sidewalks; traffic signals, select windows, bridge fixtures, harbor lamps and garage lights establish believable depth. Preserve useful dark cover for stealth. No neon/emissive visual treatment on city props, in line with the existing owner override.
- Add a real day/night cycle so heists can occur by day or night. Time of day should be consistent across the city, bridge and garage-side environment, and the road lighting should switch on appropriately. The cycle length and how a heist's starting time is chosen remain tuning decisions; night cannot be the only playable state.

## Giant yacht: version 2 first major update

- Reserve an oversized berth and open sightline now. The vessel should dwarf nearby yachts and feel almost impossible in scale: several stacked decks, a long continuous hull, helicopter deck, glass lounges, service ramp, private marina security and a skyline-scale profile.
- Its later heist should use the vessel as a proper multi-zone level (dock approach, service entry, public luxury spaces, restricted decks, security core, vault/target, escape). This is **future content**, not an instruction to build the yacht heist during the city revision.
- Owner decision Oct 1: version 1 includes only the reserved harbor space and sightlines, with no yacht exterior. Preserve space for the future yacht heist.

## Production intent and implementation choices

- Aim for **believable environmental realism**: coherent architecture, road rules, light, scale and ambient life. On phone-first Roblox, achieve this through strong silhouettes and concentrated detail at player height, streamed districts, reusable facade kits, distance simplification and selective active lights. Preserve smooth driving and existing game rules while raising visual fidelity.
- The concept is locked; these specifics remain implementation choices or owner decisions if they affect scope: final city name, precise map extent, exact heist tower placement, day/night cycle duration and how heist start time is chosen, and final number of major landmarks. Preserve the locked geography and visual direction while resolving them.
- The current code is a primitive ten-block heist city with fixed nighttime lighting and no ocean/bridge. This concept requires a substantial redesign, not only a texture pass.
